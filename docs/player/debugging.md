# Debugging lp-player — audio, seams, wrong output, frame pacing, scenes

AI debug-mode recipe for "why was there silence/a glitch at roughly time T": which `ctl`
query or probe answers it, and what that answer usually means. See
`.claude/rules/player-core.md` § Command API → "Debugging with `ctl` (AI recipe)" for the
general (not audio-specific) version this extends, and § Audio bus for the bus/meter
themselves.

## Symptom → query → likely cause

| Symptom | Query | Likely cause |
|---|---|---|
| A fade/crossfade "feels" uneven — loud, then suddenly quiet, or vice versa, even though no gap is audible | `ctl probe '{"name":"sources"}'` → compare `gain_effective_db` (what the graph thinks) against `mpv_volume_gain` (what mpv actually runs) for the source mid-ramp | **Cubic volume sag.** mpv's `volume` property (and PulseAudio/PipeWire-pulse stream volumes) are **cubic** in linear amplitude, not linear — `volume=50` measures −18.07 dB, not −6. A gain pushed through `volume` instead of `volume-gain` sags hard in the middle of a ramp. lp-player never does this (`units::mpv_volume_gain`, `MpvSource` always drives `volume-gain` and pins `volume = 100` — § Units) — but a *regression* reintroducing a `volume` write anywhere, or a new edge (a future scene/LWE volume control) copying the wrong curve, reproduces exactly this. Fix: drive the device's gain knob in dB (`volume-gain` for mpv, `units::pulse_volume_from_db`'s cubic curve only at the PulseAudio edge, never both). |
| A glitch lands right at a speed change (a playlist speed override, a live `speed` set) | `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"source."}'` around the `speed` `set`'s `t_mono_ms`; `ctl probe '{"name":"dropout","args":{"corr":"<the nearest transition, if one is running>"}}'` if a transition is also in flight then | **Speed-step micro-gap.** Changing `speed` live re-times the audio resampler; mpv needs a brief settle window before its output is trustworthy again (the legacy engine's own constant for this was `AUDIO_SETTLE`). A `probe dropout` run straddling the moment shows it as a short `micro_gaps` entry (too brief to move a whole 10 ms bin's average into `drops` territory) rather than a `drops` entry — that's the expected shape of this bug, not evidence the detector is wrong. If it widens into an actual `drops` entry, the settle window itself has regressed. |
| Total silence for the first second or so after a transition starts, then it's fine | `ctl logs '{"corr":"<corr>"}'` — look for `transition.failed {reason}` vs a `transition.started` whose `start_ms` is much later than the request; `ctl probe '{"name":"dropout","args":{"corr":"<corr>"}}'` once `done` has fired | **Ready-timeout silence.** B must produce `Ready` before the effect can start; `recipes::transition::READY_TIMEOUT` (10 s today) is the cap before it's treated as failed (the legacy renderer's equivalent cap was 3.5 s). A slow decoder start, a cold network mount, or a corrupt file right at the edge of that window reads as "nothing happened for N seconds" rather than a clean failure. `transition.failed {path, reason}` names it; if instead the transition *did* start but late, the dropout window (`transition.started` − 500 ms … `done` + 500 ms) will show real silence before `start_ms` as content, not a bug — the recipe genuinely waited. |
| A wallpaper is silent after `ctl stop`/`quit` then being restored (e.g. by the backend's `--restore`), even though it was playing fine before | `ctl probe '{"name":"sources"}'` → `mpv_pause` / `paused` on the restored source; `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"source."}'` for `source.parked`/`source.unparked` | **Paused-after-kill silence (a parked wallpaper staying paused).** A wallpaper killed mid-playback must come back **unpaused** on restore — audio follows the picture (project invariant), so a picture that's playing but a source still flagged `paused` from the kill path is silent for no visible reason. `Runtime`'s park/unpark (§ Runtime → Outputs, R60) always unpauses on unpark unless it was paused *before* the kill; if a wallpaper comes back paused that wasn't paused when it went away, that's the bug this pattern is named for. |
| **Resolved (Task 16c, R85).** A `method: frozen` (or `reveal`) transition's handoff used to have a ~15–35 ms gap of true digital silence (not a quiet ramp — a flat −96 dB floor) right as B unpauses, before B's audio jumped straight to a level already well into its 30 ms anti-click ramp (never caught gradually ramping through the gap itself) | `ctl probe '{"name":"dropout","args":{"corr":"<corr>"}}'` once `done` has fired → a `micro_gaps` entry a few ms after the effect's own end (handoff); `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"source.audio_flowing"}'` shows exactly when each onset's wait resolved (and `transition.onset_timeout` if one ever has to ramp unconfirmed) | **Real audio-out restart latency at an unpause handoff (owner's "flicker of silence" report).** B had been *paused* through the whole held period (frozen/reveal); unpausing it at handoff has to restart the mpv/PipeWire audio path, which takes longer than the ramp assumed was already flowing. **Fix (R85):** the ramp-in no longer starts at the instant of un-pause — `SourceEvent::AudioFlowing` (armed on the paused → un-paused edge; invariant #8) holds it. **Measured the signal itself is unreliable** (4 on-machine runs): `audio-pts`'s first post-un-pause change ("Current audio playback position... takes into account the audio driver delay", man mpv) is not the AO actually emitting samples — it fires anywhere 4–65 ms after un-pause, and real audio (the bus meter) then stays silent for a *further* 12–33 ms after that change specifically (one run: change at +65 ms, first real sample at +85 ms). Gating the ramp on the first change alone (tried first) reproduced the exact bug, just a few ms later. **Fixed further:** `MpvSource` requires `FLOWING_CONFIRM_MARGIN` (40 ms, measured) of real time *after* that first change before trusting it. Task 16c left a small ~5–7 ms residual `micro_gaps` entry at the same relative offset — see the next row's history; Task 16d's fast gain timer (below) closed the real-audio side of it, and R94 (further below) found the detector's own remaining share. |
| **Resolved (Task 16c found the cause, out of scope there; Task 16d fixed it; R94 found the detector's own remaining share).** A reveal/frozen A-hold's ramp-to-silence (or B's ramp-in) showed a ~5–7 ms `micro_gaps` entry (occasionally escalating to a `drops` entry) right at its tail, in *every* on-machine run at 60 Hz | Raw `meter.window` samples spanning the gap: real audio holds at a *constant*, clearly-audible level (not decaying) for the gap's whole span, then snaps to exactly −96 dB within one 1 ms sample | **History.** Task 16c ruled out requirement #2's pause-timing fix (the gap happens *before* `tr.pause_at` fires) and root-caused it to **the `Animator`'s tick-rate coarseness**: a linear-*amplitude* 30 ms ramp converted to dB concentrates almost its entire audible range into the ramp's last ~1 ms (`lin_to_db`: at 29/30 ms progress, amplitude 0.033 → −29.6 dB; at 30/30, exactly −96 dB), but at 60 Hz a 30 ms ramp got only 1–2 `Animator::tick` evaluations — mpv's `volume-gain` (and the real output) *held* whatever was last pushed for most of the ramp's final ~15 ms, then snapped straight to the final push, never smoothly passing through the intervening values. **Fixed (Task 16d, R90):** a second, independent calloop timer at ≥ 1 kHz (`runtime::GAIN_TICK_PERIOD`), armed only while a `gain.*` animation exists, evaluates and pushes every ~1 ms instead — on-machine, this closed the `full-live`/`frozen`/`retire` cases outright (0 drops/micro_gaps at both 60 Hz and 240 Hz). **One more residual, found rolling this out, also fixed:** `Runtime::gain_history` (the dropout detector's own *expected* envelope source) was still recorded once per *main tick* only — raising the real push rate to 1 kHz while the detector's model stayed at 60 Hz made the model go stale mid-ramp, producing **false** `drops`/`micro_gaps` of its own (10–30 ms, 16–52 dB) on `frozen`/`pause-resume`, worse than before. Fixed by also calling `record_gain_history()` from the fast timer whenever it pushes. **What's left is the detector's own alignment limit, not the audio (R94):** even with a real, true 1 kHz ramp and a 1 kHz-sampled expected envelope, the curve's dB value still crosses from audible to the floor within well under 1 ms of real time (see `probe/dropout.rs`'s own R94 doc for the exact numbers) — no alignment the 10 ms lag search or realistic push/capture jitter offers can land exactly on that edge. **Fixed (R94):** the micro-gap pass now requires the expected level to stay audible for a sample's *whole* ±2 ms neighbourhood, not just at that one instant — a ramp edge never has that, a real glitch does. If a micro-gap still appears after all of this, check whether it sits at a ramp edge (the expected level within ±2 ms of it crosses −45 dB) before treating it as real. |
| **Resolved (Task 16d — the detector, not the audio).** Intermittent `frozen` findings right at effect start: a 4–5 ms / ~61 dB `micro_gaps` entry at `at_ms` ≈ 532 with `lag_ms` 10, or (rarer, seen @240 Hz) a 30 ms / ~73 dB `drops` entry at `at_ms` 500 with `lag_ms` 40 — "A's whole anti-click ramp observed silent" | `ctl probe '{"name":"dropout","args":{"corr":"<corr>","dump":"/tmp/d.json"}}'` → the analysis' complete inputs (1 ms meter samples, both expected envelopes, the recorded gain schedule, the report), replayable offline; compare the meter's ramp against the schedule's ramp directly (`dropout.sh` writes one next to its kept logs on any finding) | **Detector alignment — three causes, all fixed:** (1) the meter samples were binned on the *absolute* 10 ms grid while `expected` starts at the window's own arbitrary sub-10 ms phase φ (`transition.started` − 500 ms), so "lag 10" really meant 10 − φ and the micro-gap pass then shifted the fine envelope a full 10 ms (φ ≈ 8 ms → 8 ms off at the ramp's steepest point: exactly the 532/4 ms/61 dB signature); (2) a 10 ms lag step can't align a steep edge at all (R96: refined to 1 ms on the fine envelope); (3) the auto-trigger snapshotted the meter at `transition.done`, so the capture stopped at B's ramp while `expected` ran 500 ms on — a lag that pushed B's ramp bins off the captured data scored better (40 ms, the last lag still clearing the R77 guard) and A's ramp bins then paired with true silence (now deferred to `done + WINDOW_POST + CAPTURE_MARGIN`). **Evidence it was never real:** 24 reproduced frozen transitions (12 @ 60 Hz, 12 @ 240 Hz, incl. an exact repeat of the 40 ms / 30 ms-drop case): the meter **and an independent `parec` capture of `lp-player-bus.monitor`** show A's full 30 ms linear-amplitude ramp every time (−6 → −30 dB in 12–16 ms, silent at effect start + 31–35 ms); the gain pushes land at ~1 ms steps from t0 + 1.3 to t0 + 30.3 ms; A's mpv `pause` is requested at t0 + 45.9 ms (after the ramp + `AUDIO_SETTLE_TAIL`); B's onset ramps 2 ms after its first push. The fixed detector on the same data: `lag_ms` 1–2, `fit_db` 0.05–0.19, no findings — and an instant cut injected at effect start into the same real window is still a 30 ms drop. |

These rows are the exact bugs the legacy (pre-lp-player) renderer had, or (the last row) a
real player-side latency this detector surfaced, when the original
`scripts/lp_audiowatch.py` (2026-10-20) was first pointed at it — proof this
reference-based technique finds real issues, not just confirms things are fine. It's now
native to lp-player as `probe dropout` (below) instead of an external sink monitor.

## The tools

- **`ctl probe '{"name":"dropout","args":{"corr":"tr-N"}}'`** — re-runs the
  reference-based dropout detector for a transition that has already reached
  `transition.done` (find a recent `corr` with `ctl logs
  '{"kind_prefix":"transition."}'`). Compares what the bus actually output (the meter)
  against what the sources + the *recorded* gain schedule say should have played, over
  `transition.started` − 500 ms … `.done` + 500 ms. Replies `{"status":"pending","corr"}`
  at once (it decodes with `ffmpeg` and analyses off the main loop — never blocks the
  player) and delivers the result as a `probe.dropout {corr, report}` event (`ctl
  subscribe probe.` to see it live). Add `"dump":"/abs/path.json"` to also write every
  input the analysis saw (meter samples, both expected envelopes, the gain schedule,
  the sources' rebased positions) plus the report — the evidence for "real gap or the
  detector's own alignment" (Task 16d). `report`: `lag_ms` (0–250, the best alignment found — 1 ms
  resolution, R96: the 10 ms-step search refined ±10 ms on the 1 ms envelope),
  `fit_db` (how good that alignment is — large means the window had little reliable
  overlap, not necessarily a bug), `offset_db` (a level mismatch, e.g. a miscalibrated
  bus gain — not itself a dropout), `drops` (10 ms-bin-granularity real gaps),
  `micro_gaps` (finer, sub-bin gaps too short to move a whole bin's average — see the
  speed-step row above for what a *legitimate* one looks like), `unmeasured` (Phase 2
  Task 14: stretches the bus meter itself missed — a timestamp jump, then −96 blocks from
  its non-real-time capture — left out of the analysis; a few per hundred switches under
  load is the meter, not the audio; a drop bin also needs audible expected neighbours, so a
  ramp edge a few ms late is never one). When debug mode is on
  (`serve --debug`, or `set player.debug true` live) this also runs **automatically**
  after every `transition.done` — once the window's 500 ms post-roll has really been
  captured (Task 16d: `done + 500 ms + CAPTURE_MARGIN`, so ≈ 0.6–0.8 s after `done`) — (a
  plain source retire's 30 ms anti-click ramp and an R88 pause gate record their own
  windows too, probed on demand: `source.retire`'s / `player.pause_gate`'s log event
  carries the `corr` it minted, so `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"source.retire"}'` finds one
  to probe), so `ctl
  subscribe probe.` during normal use already surfaces a dropout the moment one happens.
  **Two different "nothing to report" shapes, don't confuse them:** the on-demand verb
  (`ctl probe '{"name":"dropout",…}'`) replies **synchronously** with an `unsupported`
  *error* only when it can't even start (no `transition.done`/retire window recorded yet,
  or the meter is down — the hint names which); once it *does* start, the result always
  arrives later as the async `probe.dropout` **event**, and that event's own `skipped`
  field (not an error) covers a start-time problem the auto-trigger can hit with nobody
  watching synchronously — meter down, or (R79) a third decode already in flight
  (`skipped: "busy"`; at most 2 run at once — try again shortly). A `report.fit:
  "insufficient"` (R77) is a third, still-different case: the analysis *ran*, but didn't
  have enough reliably-paired bins to trust any lag/offset — not an error, not skipped,
  just "couldn't check," with `drops`/`micro_gaps` both empty on purpose.
  **Not modeled:** a second audible wallpaper on another output sharing the same bus —
  the expected mix only ever weights the sources belonging to *this* corr's own window
  (one transition's A/B, or one retiring source), never anything else concurrently
  feeding the bus.
- **`ctl probe '{"name":"meter.window","args":{"last_ms":1000}}'`** (or
  `{"from_ms":…,"to_ms":…}`) — the bus meter's raw 1 ms dBFS samples (what the player's
  audio bus actually output, and nothing else — not the default sink, not another app).
  `median_db`/`min_db`/`max_db`/`max_gap_ms` first; add the full `samples` array only when
  you need to look at the shape yourself (it's left out with `"samples":false`).
- **`ctl probe '{"name":"bus"}'`** — is there a bus at all (`state`, `device`), its
  measured `latency_ms`, which sink it's really linked to (`target`, `overridden` if a
  session policy like EasyEffects moved it), and the meter's own health
  (`meter.available`/`streaming`/`dropped`). Check this *first* whenever `dropout` or
  `meter.window` says `unsupported`/`skipped` — it names the reason.
- **`ctl probe '{"name":"sources"}'`** — every live source's `gain_effective_db` (what
  the graph computed) next to `gains` (its own slots) and the synchronous mpv reads
  (`mpv_volume_gain`, `mpv_pause`, `mpv_audio_device`, `mpv_audio_delay`,
  `requests_in_flight`) — the single best place to see "what the graph wants" vs "what
  mpv is actually doing" side by side.
- **`ctl probe '{"name":"frames"}'`** — present timing per output; a dropout that
  coincides with a `missed` frame or an idle↔paced flip is more likely a GPU/compositor
  stall than an audio bug.
- **`ctl logs '{"corr":"tr-N"}'`** — one transition's whole story in order
  (`scheduled`→`started`→`handoff`→`done`, or where it stopped:
  `cancelled`/`failed {reason}`); `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"audio."}'` for the bus's own
  retarget/latency history; `ctl logs '{"min_level":"warn"}'` for everything that went
  wrong recently, unfiltered.

## Seams, wrong output, frame pacing, lifecycle

| Symptom | Query | Likely cause |
|---|---|---|
| A visible seam/flash at a switch — a black or transparent frame between A and B | `ctl logs '{"corr":"<corr>"}'` (did it start/hand off normally?); `ctl capture '{"target":"out:<c>","path":"/tmp/x.png"}'` mid-transition; `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"effects."}'` | **An effect that doesn't compile on this driver** (`effects.compile_failed {id}` logged once) now draws as a *cut at the midpoint* (`gfx/compose.rs`, final review M3) — never a black screen; before, the target was left transparent every frame. A transparent frame with no such event is a source with no texture yet (R19: a missing texture is transparent) — B shown before its `Ready` is a recipe bug. |
| The wrong wallpaper on one output (or the old one comes back) | `ctl state` → `lay:<c>:main.content`, `cur:<c>`, `parked`; `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"output."}'` (`output.added {content}`) | Hotplug rules (player-core.md § Runtime → Outputs): a new output adopts the live transition, else `cur:` of the first output, else the parked wallpaper. With **no output at all**, every `transition` replaces the parked wallpaper (I4) — the *last* target shows on replug; `transition.scheduled {parked: true}`, an `align: end` one hands off only then. |
| Rotation raced ahead (or stopped) while the monitors were off | `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"transition.scheduled"}'` (`parked: true` entries); the adapter log (`aligned switch … switching in N s`) | Pre-fix the player refused every switch with no output and the adapter retried each second (I4). Now refusals can't happen there, and the adapter backs off 1 → 30 s on any non-handoff and keeps an aligned wait alive while `state.outputs` is empty. |
| Missed frames / a hitch on every output at each switch | `ctl probe '{"name":"loop","args":{"reset":true}}'` before, `ctl probe '{"name":"loop"}'` after → `max_busy_ms`, `max_open_ms`; `ctl probe '{"name":"frames"}'` → `paced.missed` | A switch's synchronous loop work (I5): the mpv core is created on the `lp-open` worker (2–8 ms off the loop); what remains on the loop is the render context + property applies + `loadfile` (`max_open_ms`, 1–8 ms) and the **first render** of the new context (shader compile/LUTs: 6–20 ms with the shader cache + `dither=ordered`). Measured per switch: 32–54 ms before, 8–26 ms after (`tests/api.rs::i5_switch_loop_busy`). A spike well above that is new work on the loop. The Phase 2 render worker removes the first render too. |
| A hitch at every switch far above the I5 numbers — 60–100 ms (even with `hwdec=no`) or 230–320 ms (`hwdec=auto`) | `ctl probe '{"name":"loop"}'` after a switch → `max_busy_ms` vs `max_open_ms`; `ctl probe '{"name":"sources"}'` → `mpv_hwdec` (what we asked mpv for), `mpv_hwdec_current` (what it decodes with); the player's environment (`/proc/<pid>/environ`: `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`, `DISPLAY`) | **Hwdec init on the loop (Task 20d, R107), two NVIDIA traps.** (1) `max_open_ms` ≈ `max_busy_ms` ≈ 65–90 ms: mpv's render context loading the **CUDA GL interop** (a CUDA context) inside `mpv_render_context_create` — mpv's default `gpu-hwdec-interop=auto` does it eagerly whenever libmpv can load `libcuda.so.1` (the flake wrapper's `LD_LIBRARY_PATH=/run/opengl-driver/lib`, or once libavutil loaded it for `nvdec-copy`), whatever `hwdec` says. libmpv's own RUNPATH lacks `/run/opengl-driver/lib` (nixpkgs adds it to `bin/mpv` only), which is why the dev binary logs `ffmpeg: Cannot load libcuda.so.1` once per source. (2) `max_open_ms` small but `max_busy_ms` 230–320 ms with `mpv_hwdec_current: vulkan-copy`: each new core's `vkCreateDevice` (mpv's core thread) holds an NVIDIA driver lock our GL calls wait on (strace: the loop in `FUTEX_LOCK_PI` for the whole device creation) — only when no X server is reachable (`DISPLAY` unset/dead, e.g. headless tests or an Xwayland-less session; with the session's Xwayland: 7–22 ms). **Fixed:** every core runs `gpu-hwdec-interop=no`, and `sources::mpv::mpv_hwdec` maps the graph value to copy modes (`auto` → `nvdec-copy,vaapi-copy,auto-copy`, `nvdec` → `nvdec-copy`, …): 8–42 ms per switch in every mode except an explicit `vulkan`/`vulkan-copy` without X (the driver limit above, unchanged). **Since 2026-10-27 the render worker decodes zero-copy:** a source rendered there (`render_mode: worker`) loads the worker's interop (`hwdec_interop: cuda` on NVIDIA) and `mpv_hwdec_current: nvdec` is right; the init runs on an `lp-render` lane (`render_worker.lanes[].attach_ms_max`, `render_worker.attach {lane, ms}` events). An interop mode or `gpu-hwdec-interop` ≠ `no` on a **loop**-rendered source (`render_mode: loop`) is the regression; so is `max_busy_ms` growing with the worker on. A/B: `LP_PLAYER_HWDEC_INTEROP=off` (copy modes, one lane). A playing wallpaper that hitches while the next one opens: two live sources shared a lane (`lanes[].live` ≥ 2 — a fourth concurrent wallpaper, or `LP_PLAYER_RENDER_LANES=1`). Guard: `tests/api.rs::t20d_hwdec_switch_never_stalls_the_loop` (both environments, `DISPLAY` removed, both render modes). |
| A wallpaper stayed frozen/silent after a stop or a new wallpaper, though `/current` said playing | `ctl get '{"path":"player.paused"}'` | The player-level pause belongs to the session (I1): `stop` clears it, the backend clears it for a new session (Apply / ApplyPlaylist / a cold Launch). If it's still `true` after either, that's a regression. |
| Pause turned itself on/off after a crash restart or a stall | `ctl get '{"path":"player.paused"}'` before/after; the adapter log (`re-applied effective settings … (player.paused = …, kept)`) | The player is the only authority for pause (persisted in its snapshot; I3). The backend's R81 reapply reads it and never re-asserts a copy (a keybind toggles from a separate CLI process; a copy goes stale). |
| No wallpaper audio at all after the audio server restarted | `ctl probe '{"name":"bus"}'` (`state`, `reason`); `ctl probe '{"name":"sources"}'` → `mpv_audio_device`, `mpv_current_ao`; `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"audio.bus."}'` | I7: the bus re-creates itself (`audio.bus.down` → `audio.bus.reconnecting {attempt}` → `audio.bus.up {reconnected: true, generation}`, backoff 0.5 → 10 s) and every source is re-routed (`audio.bus.sources_rerouted`: `audio-device`, then the audio track re-selected — a dead server takes mpv's AO with it and mpv deselects the track), ramping back in on flowing audio (`onset.started {slot: "pause"}`). `mpv_current_ao: null` after `up` = the re-select didn't happen. |
| A transition never starts on `align: end` | `ctl state` (phase `waiting_start`); `ctl logs '{"corr":"<corr>"}'` | A's end never comes (it errored, stalled, has no duration): after A's remaining time at its speed + `ALIGN_SLACK` (10 s) it starts anyway with `transition.align_fallback` (M2); an A `Error` counts as ended at once. While the player is paused the wait is frozen on purpose (invariant #7). |
| RSS grows per switch — tens of MiB per switch at first, then a slow creep; VRAM flat; RSS doesn't come back down once switching stops | `ctl probe '{"name":"memory"}'` → `malloc.in_use_bytes + mmap_bytes` (live) vs `malloc.free_bytes` (freed but retained), `malloc.arenas`, `policy`; `ctl probe '{"name":"memory","args":{"trim":true}}'` → `trim.proc_after.rss_kib` (what one `malloc_trim(0)` gives back) | **Allocator retention, not a leak (Task 20b).** Live flat + free growing (+ a trim that gives back a lot) = glibc keeping freed memory: its *dynamic* mmap threshold rises past a 1080p frame buffer (~3 MiB) once the first mpv core is destroyed, so every later core's frame pools/demuxer blocks come from ~57 per-thread arenas that each keep their peak. Measured (200 hermetic switches, 2 × 1080p): RSS 159 → 922 MiB with live flat at ~300 MiB and free 1 → 614 MiB; `hwdec=auto` (`vulkan-copy` here) 1.2–1.6 GiB; the Task 20 live soak's 6.4 MiB/min was this. `mem::apply_policy` (a fixed 128 KiB threshold + `malloc_trim(0)` after each reaper batch, § Memory in `.claude/rules/player-core.md`) holds it at 364 → 380 MiB over 300 switches. `policy.applied: false` = off (`LP_PLAYER_MALLOC=glibc`, the A/B switch). If *live* grows instead, it's a real leak: `loop` → `bounds`, then `reaper_pending`/`dying_sources`/`opening` here. Guard: `tests/api.rs::t20b_switch_loop_rss_stays_flat`; soak: `battery.sh soak --hermetic` (every sample carries `malloc` + `switches`). |
| In-use heap grows per switch (`memory` probe `malloc.in_use_bytes` climbs ~1–2 MiB/min in a live soak while it stays flat headless; RSS follows) | Per minute: in-use delta vs `transitions_done_total` delta — if minutes *without* a switch grow as fast, it is per present, not per switch. Then `ctl probe '{"name":"frames"}'` → `explicit_sync`, `default_queue_dispatched`; a private Wayland player with a long colour fade (heap per `totals.presents`); `heaptrack --record-only` (built from the flake's nixpkgs: `nix build --inputs-from . nixpkgs#heaptrack`, and `LD_LIBRARY_PATH=/run/opengl-driver/lib` or EGL won't init under it) and `heaptrack_print -d` of a short vs a long run | **An event queue nobody dispatches (Task 20e).** NVIDIA egl-wayland2 ≤ 1.0.2 with explicit sync keeps its `wl_buffer`s on a swapchain queue it never dispatches; every `wl_buffer.release` (one per present) stays queued — ~224 B per present, under `wl_closure_init` ← `wl_display_read_events` in heaptrack. Fixed by `__NV_DISABLE_EXPLICIT_SYNC=1` (`wl::apply_driver_env`, unless the caller set it — `explicit_sync.source: env` with `value: "0"` brings it back) and the default-queue drain (`Wayland::process`; upstream's egl-wayland2 fix moves the buffers there). Growth with `explicit_sync.value` "1" points elsewhere: another C library's queue, or a Rust collection (`loop` → `bounds`). Guard: `LP_PLAYER_TEST_WAYLAND=<socket> cargo test --test api t20e`. |
| Memory grows over a day of rotation | `ctl probe '{"name":"loop"}'` → `bounds` | Every per-source collection is reported there (`gain_history_tracks/samples`, `anim_entries`, `dropout_windows`, …). `GainHistory` (C1) keeps dead sources' tracks only `DEAD_LINGER` (30 s, for post-roll analyses) and never more than 64 tracks / 131 072 samples; anything else that keeps growing is a leak. |

## Scenes (the `lp-scene-worker` processes)

Start with `ctl probe '{"name":"workers"}'` — one entry per scene source: `state`
(`not_spawned`, `starting`, `running`, `restarting N`, `broken`, `stopped`), `pid`, `ready`, `last_error`,
`fps_target`/`fps_actual`, `latched`, `stale_reuses`, `restarts_60s`/`total_restarts`, `sync`
(`fence` or `fdpoll`), `size`/`native`, `audio {…}`, `loudness {…}`, `throttle`, `vram_stopped`, `broken`,
`preview`. Add **`"args":{"log_tail":true}`** (`ctl probe '{"name":"workers","args":{"log_tail":true}}'`) to get
each worker's last 50 output lines — LWE's own stderr (missing assets, shader warnings, `Replaced … with compat
…`) plus the worker's `log` messages, kept across restarts and after a fatal stop. That tail is the first thing to
read for any "this scene looks or behaves wrong" report; `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"worker."}'`,
`'{"kind_prefix":"scene."}'` and `'{"kind_prefix":"source."}'` give the process-lifecycle story. A broken or
crashed scene also leaves a record: `<state>/crashes/<scene-id>-<unix_ms>.json` (`$XDG_STATE_HOME`, default
`~/.local/state/livepaper/lp-player/crashes/`; the newest 20 are kept) with the signal or exit code, the restart
count and the log tail. `lp-player scene-check` answers "can scenes play here at all" (worker binary, LWE built in,
WE's assets dir).

| Symptom | Query | Likely cause |
|---|---|---|
| A scene shows **black** (or its output keeps the previous wallpaper) and nothing plays | `ctl probe '{"name":"workers","args":{"log_tail":true}}'` → `state`, `ready`, `last_error`, `log_tail`; `ctl logs '{"min_level":"warn"}'` for `source.error` / `worker.*` / `scene.assets`; `lp-player scene-check` | **It never produced a frame.** (1) `state: not_spawned` + a `last_error` naming `LP_WE_ASSETS_DIR` / `player.scene_assets` — Wallpaper Engine's `assets/` wasn't found (no worker is spawned on purpose: never a 1×1 render loop); the backend then doesn't offer scenes (`hello.capabilities` has no `scene`). (2) A fatal worker error (`load failed: …`: the folder is gone, still downloading from Steam, or a truncated `scene.pkg`; `renderer build failed …`: LWE couldn't build the scene) — the worker stays up, idle, with **no restart and no crash record by design**; the `log_tail` has LWE's reason (the corpus' `load_failed` / `renderer_failed` classes — all LWE-core bugs, Phase 6, `docs/player/reports/scene-corpus.md`). (3) The scene loaded but draws nothing: an object whose effect failed to compile disappears (`Failed to setup object …` in the tail). (4) `state: broken` — see "crash loop" below; a broken scene shows its **preview** (`preview.shown`), not black, unless the item has none (`scene.preview_failed`). (5) `ctl capture '{"target":"<scene source id>","path":"/tmp/x.png"}'` black while `latched` climbs = LWE itself drew black; `latched` stuck at 0 while `ticks_sent` climbs and `frames_received` stays 0 = the worker isn't answering (the heartbeat kills and restarts it within 2 s). |
| A scene is **frozen** (picture not moving) though the wallpaper "is playing" | `ctl probe '{"name":"workers"}'` → compare `latched` (should grow at `fps_actual`) with `stale_reuses`, `ticks_sent`, `frames_received`, `queued`, `paused`, `resuming`; `ctl get '{"path":"player.paused"}'`; `ctl get '{"path":"player.vram_low_active"}'` | Read the counters as a chain: a tick goes out (`ticks_sent`) → the worker answers (`frames_received`) → the frame's fence has signalled and it latches (`latched`). **`stale_reuses` climbing while `latched` is flat** = the worker renders but its frames' fences aren't signalling — the GPU is starved (a game, a heavy scene; check `vram`, `scene.throttled`, `gpu_ms_p50`): the compositor keeps the last frame instead of waiting (invariant #10), which is correct. **`ticks_sent` flat** = the source is paused: `paused: true` (item or player pause — a frozen frame is the intended behaviour), `player.vram_low_active`, parked outputs, or `resuming` after a crash (frozen until the restarted worker's first frame, then it continues from the held time). **`frames_received` flat while `ticks_sent` grows** = the worker is hung (`worker.hung` follows within `HANG_TIMEOUT`, 2 s, then SIGKILL + restart). A scene that moves but slowly: `throttle` ≠ null (below). |
| A scene is **silent** (or too quiet/loud) while it is visibly playing | `ctl probe '{"name":"workers"}'` → `audio {route, gain_db, no_audio, has_audio, speed, flows, envelope_dbfs}`; `ctl probe '{"name":"sources"}'` → its `gain_effective_db`; `ctl probe '{"name":"bus"}'`; `pw-link -l` or `pw-dump` for a node named `lp-player-scene.<id>` linked into `lp-player-bus` | **`audio.has_audio: false`** — the scene has no sound (LWE installed no audio stream; many scenes are silent; no `lp-player-scene.<id>` node exists by design, and no loudness is ever learned). `no_audio: true` or `gain_db` ≈ −96 — the item/global mute, a per-item volume of 0, another `gain.*` slot, or a transition ramp still in flight. `route: null` = the player runs `--ao null`. A node that exists but is **not linked to `lp-player-bus`** — `ctl probe '{"name":"bus"}'` `target`/`overridden` (a session policy moved it), or PipeWire restarted (`audio.bus.sources_rerouted` re-routes scenes too; `audio.flows` stays false until the rebuilt stream flows). Speed ≠ 1 is pitch-kept time-stretching (WSOLA): a **debug** worker at ≠ 1× misses 64-frame audio cycles (1–2 ms holes) — judge release builds only. An onset that never confirms logs `onset.started {reason: timeout}` / `transition.onset_timeout`: check that the worker said `AudioFlowing` (`source.audio_flowing`). |
| A scene keeps restarting — a **crash loop**, then "broken" / "Scene crashed — won't be played" | `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"worker."}'` (`worker.exited {signal or code, uptime_ms}` → `worker.restart {attempt, why}` → `worker.broken {reason, record}`); the newest `<state>/crashes/*.json` (`signal`, `restarts`, `uptime_ms`, `log_tail` — the worker's last lines, LWE's included); `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"source.broken"}'` | **The restart budget is 3 per 60 s** (`restarts_60s`); the 4th crash in the window makes the scene `broken`: the source holds its last frame (or its preview), silent, and `source.broken {scene_id, reason, record, signal, shows}` goes to the backend, which marks the item crashed (skipped unless whitelisted) and advances a rotation. `signal: 6` (SIGABRT) = LWE threw past its C API (most corpus failures: an unparseable `project.json`/`scene.json` field); `11` = a segfault in LWE's build or render (particles, effects); a reason starting **"GL context lost"** = the GPU reset (suspend/resume, VT switch, driver purge — one reset is one restart, innocent); `hung` = no frame within `HANG_TIMEOUT` (`LP_SCENE_WORKER_HANG_MS` shortens it in tests). A player restart does **not** reset the budget: a broken scene is restored broken (as its preview, no worker) — re-applying the same item needs a new source (a `transition`; a same-path `set` is a no-op). Worker crashes skip the core handler (`RLIMIT_CORE` 1, so no systemd-coredump popup); `LP_SCENE_WORKER_COREDUMP=1` keeps cores. To reproduce an LWE crash without the player: `lp-scene-worker --lwe-smoke <scene dir> --assets <WE assets> --out <dir>` (under `gdb`, `catch throw`). |
| A scene plays at a **lower fps than set**, or `scene.throttled` is in the log | `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"scene.throttle"}'` and `'{"kind_prefix":"scene.unthrottled"}'` (`scene.throttled {fps_from, fps_to, demand, gpu_ms, fps_actual, missed_pct}`, `scene.throttle_eased`, `scene.unthrottled {reason: recovered, off or demand}`); `ctl probe '{"name":"workers"}'` → `throttle {cap, demand, since_ms}`, `fps_demand` vs `fps_target` vs `fps_actual`, `gpu_ms_p50/p95`; `ctl get '{"path":"player.scene_throttle"}'`, `ctl get '{"path":"out:<c>.scene_fps"}'` | **The GPU budget doing its job.** The scene's worker uses ≥ 40 % of the GPU (`gpu_ms_p50 × fps_actual ≥ 400 ms/s`) *and* the compositor's frames on an output it shows missed > 2 % (or the scene itself latched < 90 % of its tick rate over 2 s): its rate is halved (floor 10 fps), then eased back ×1.25 per calm 5 s once misses fall below 0.5 %. `throttle: null` and `fps_actual` still below `fps_target` = not the budget: the GPU can't keep up (see "frozen"), or the output's present gate (`out:<c>.scene_fps`, default 30; 0 = the output's refresh) is lower than expected — it is per output, and one worker serves all outputs. `player.scene_throttle false` = a fixed fps (any cap lifts at once, `reason: off`). A cap that never lifts although the load is gone: `ctl probe '{"name":"frames"}'` still shows missed frames > 0.5 % — find that other cause. |
| A scene (or **every** wallpaper) turned into a still image / was **stopped** — "VRAM" in the UI | `ctl probe '{"name":"vram"}'` → `probe` (`nvml`, `drm`, `nvidia-smi`, `fake` or `none`), `sample {total_mib, free_mib, procs}`, `rule.state` (`normal`, `pending`, `low`, `recovering`), `hold {mode, phase}`, `scenes [{id, pid, used_mib, over_ms, stopped}]`, `scene_limit_mib`; `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"vram."}'` and `'{"kind_prefix":"scene.vram"}'` | Two separate rules. **Per-scene limit** (`player.scene_vram_limit_mib`, 0 = off): `scene.vram_exceeded {used_mib, limit_mib}` — that one worker used more than the limit for > 2 s and was stopped (a clean `quit`, **never a crash**: no record, not `broken`; `workers.vram_stopped` set, `source.error` ending `(vram_exceeded)`); its last frame stays, the backend advances a rotation and does not mark the item crashed. A new `path` starts it again; raising the limit does not revive a stopped one. **System low-VRAM hold** (`player.vram_low_mode`, default `stop`): free VRAM stayed under max(512 MiB, 5 % of the card) for 3 s (a game filled it) → `vram.low` → the player pauses (`player.vram_low_active` true; the user's `player.paused` is untouched) and in `stop` mode drops every video/image/scene's decoder or worker (`vram.stopped`, `source.suspended`; a still of the last frame is shown). It releases only when free VRAM — *not counting what our own stop freed* — is back at ≥ low + max(1 GiB, 10 %) for 10 s (`vram.recovered` → `vram.resuming` → `vram.resumed`; each source reopens at its position, a scene on a new worker pid). A flapping card doubles the resume hold (≤ 160 s). **`probe: none`** (the reason is in the probe) = nothing can be measured, so neither rule ever acts — Intel discrete cards expose no total; `LP_PLAYER_VRAM_PROBE` forces a probe or `none`. A hold that never ends: after 30 s without a sample it releases by itself (`reason: no_sample`). |
| A scene's **loudness is never learned** (no `loudnessI` for it in the library index; it stays at its raw level) | `ctl probe '{"name":"workers"}'` → `loudness {seconds, session_s, seed_s, played_s, lufs, done, capped, window_s, cap_s, received}` and `audio.has_audio`; `ctl logs '{"kind_prefix":"scene.loudness"}'` (`scene.loudness {id, lufs, peak_dbfs, seconds, session_s, seed_s, complete, capped, state}` at the window, each further window, the end and the cap) | Learning needs **60 s of audible play** (`LP_PLAYER_LOUDNESS_WINDOW_S` in tests), measured on the worker's pre-gain stream. It never runs for a scene without sound (`has_audio: false`) or on a stepped clock (no sound plays); paused, player-paused and `no_audio` seconds aren't counted (`played_s` > `seconds`); digital silence or anything under −70 LUFS has no gated block, so nothing is ever stored — by design. Switched away before the window ends: ≥ a third of it (20 s) reports a **partial** (`complete: false`; the backend keeps it until a complete one), less reports nothing. Later plays continue the stored measurement (`loudness_seed` = the index's `loudnessState`; `seed_s` > 0 in the probe) — a source with `seed_s` 0 for a learned scene means the backend sent no seed (an entry learned before 2026-10-28 has no state: it re-learns). `capped: true` = 30 min of audible play measured in all: nothing more is measured, by design. The index's `loudnessI` moves only by ≥ 0.5 LU (adapter log `stored (< 0.5 LU: not applied)`). A compositor restart loses what the live source measured since its last report. If `scene.loudness` fired but the library has no `loudnessI`, the fault is backend-side (it subscribes and stores: `.claude/rules/player-adapter.md` § Scenes → "Loudness", `library.md` § Loudness); legacy LWE scenes are never normalized. |
